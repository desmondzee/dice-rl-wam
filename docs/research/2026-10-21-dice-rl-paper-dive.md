## 1. Conditioning of residual and critic

- **Residual:** `a_{t:t+h-1} = π_pre(s_t, z) + s_θ(s_t, z)`, `z ~ N(0, I)` (paper Eq. 2). Rationale: "Because π_pre(s_t,z) is deterministic for a fixed latent noise z, conditioning the residual policy s_θ(s_t,z) on the same z makes the correction explicitly aware of the particular base action chunk" (paper §4). No image enters the residual directly; for pixels `s` is the frozen BC encoder feature: "we freeze the observation encoder learned during BC pretraining, using it to map high-dimensional observations into a compact latent feature space for RL learning" (§4). Code: actor MLP input is `cond_steps*obs_dim + horizon*action_dim` (state ⊕ z flattened), `model/rl/distill_residual_rl.py:49-83`.
- **Critic:** chunk critic `Q_φ(s_t, a_{t:t+h-1})` with h-step bootstrapping (Eq. 4, §4 "Action chunking"); 10-critic ensemble, min reduction (Table 2, Appendix B). No z in the critic in the paper.
- **Ablations r(s,z) vs r(s,a_base), Q(s,a) vs Q(s,z,a): not reported in the paper.** Both exist as code toggles only: `condition_residual_on_base_action` (`model/rl/distill_residual_rl.py:260,941-946`) and `q_depends_on_noise` (`:107,128-131,239`). Every released config sets the former `false` and never sets the latter (default `False`), so the released recipe is `r(s,z)` and `Q(s,a)`; the config comment "Critic: Q(s,z,a)" (`cfg/robomimic/finetune/tool_hang/ft_distill_residual_flow_mlp.yaml:139`) is stale.

## 2. Pixel experiments

Yes. Robomimic Can/Square/Transport/Tool Hang in both state and pixel (Fig. 2, top/bottom rows); LIBERO-10 pixel multitask (Appendix A, Fig. 10, 100 rollouts/task, multitask BC on 500 demos) and LIBERO-10 with π0 (Fig. 11, SFT on 30 demos/task then RL); real robot (ViT-B/32 CLIP encoder diffusion policy, Appendix D).

- **Prior (sim pixel):** per-view ResNet-18 trained from scratch, random crop train / center crop eval (Tool Hang 216 of 240, Square/Can 84 of 96, Transport full 96), features concatenated with proprio and linearly projected; 1D U-Net flow head, channel mults {1,2,2}, width 128, FiLM; flow steps 10 (20 Tool Hang); horizon 8 (Transport, Tool Hang) or 4 (Can, Square) (Appendix C, Table 1).
- **Residual/critic inputs (pixel):** frozen encoder features ⊕ proprio. Code: `DistillResidualRLImgModel.get_action` calls `forward_from_features(features=state, init_noise=noise)` and feeds the same augmented `state` to actor and critic (`model/rl/distill_residual_rl_img.py:89-131`). Proprio dims 18/9/9/9 (Table 1); configs `obs_dim: 9` (Can/Square/Tool Hang) and `18` (Transport).
- **`VisionResNetFlowMatchingMLP` is used by released configs:** all four `cfg/robomimic/finetune/*/ft_distill_residual_flow_unet_img.yaml` load `pretrained_bc_policy_{env}_img` trained by `cfg/robomimic/pretrain/*/pre_flow_matching_unet_img.yaml`, whose `model.network._target_` is `model.flow_matching.vision_resnet_mlp_flow_matching.VisionResNetFlowMatchingMLP` (e.g. `cfg/robomimic/pretrain/tool_hang/pre_flow_matching_unet_img.yaml:82-84`).
- **Numbers:** the paper gives curves only (Fig. 2), no numeric table for either modality. Text claims: "first RL finetuning method to reach ≥ 90% success on Tool Hang from either state or pixel inputs using only 50 demonstrations", and "Starting from a Tool Hang pretrained BC policy with 45% success, DICE-RL surpasses 90% success within roughly 2000 online episodes" (§5.1). Per-task pixel-vs-state finals are **not reported numerically**.

## 3. Distribution shift / generalisation

- **No OOD protocol.** Evaluation is "300 held-out test configurations" (Fig. 2 caption). In code these are fixed reset seeds 10000..10299 (`agent/finetune/train_distill_residual_flow_agent.py:119,466-467`; `script/eval_rl_checkpoint.py:147`) drawn from the same robosuite reset distribution as training (`env/gym_utils/wrapper/robomimic_lowdim.py:99-135`). Not unseen object layouts, perturbations, or visual changes.
- **Robustness analysis (§5.3, Fig. 7):** (left) normalized pairwise latent-state divergence `c_x(t) = ||s_t - s'_t||² / ||s_0 - s'_0||²` (Eq. 11) between rollouts from nearby demo anchor pairs on Tool Hang (pixel), RL vs BC vs expert; (right) success rate on Transport (pixel) under action-noise injection with probability 0.1 to 0.9. Both are figures; no numbers in text. The authors state: "We do not claim that RL finetuning improves out-of-distribution generalization beyond the pretrained BC policy ... our results indicate improved robustness to small, on-trajectory perturbations" (§5.3).
- **Real robot eval randomisation (§5.4):** EE pose ±2 cm, ±10° per axis; BeltAssembly board ±10 cm, ±15° planar; 30 trials per checkpoint. This is the training/eval distribution, not a shift test.
- Pretraining-data axes studied: PH vs MH demos (Fig. 3), checkpoint epoch (Fig. 4), 50–150 demos on Transport (Fig. 18).

## 4. Why noise-space residuals; sharpening measurements

Claims (§1, §4): residual on frozen prior "preserves the pretrained flow policy's expressive, stochastic action generation", avoids denoising inside RL, allows "reparameterized policy-gradient updates through the residual", and gives "controllable exploration within the demonstrations' support" via the `β||s_θ||²` penalty (Eq. 3). Multi-sample training over K latents "lets the residual improve the entire latent-induced action distribution of π_pre instead of overfitting to one draw" (§4). DSRL framing: DICE-RL "can both optimize the latent noise and apply a learnable residual action correction" (§5.1) — latent optimisation in practice is best-of-N selection only.

Measured: (a) **sharpening** — ΔV vs ΔH at demo anchor states, "states with larger value improvements exhibit larger entropy drops" (Fig. 5); running ΔH/ΔV along a Tool Hang rollout, drops at pre-insertion/insertion (Fig. 6) and at BeltAssembly pulley transitions (Fig. 9). Entropy = per-coordinate histogram entropy, 50 bins over [-1,1], averaged over hd coordinates (§5.2). (b) **Contraction** — Eq. 11 divergence (Fig. 7 left). (c) **Finetunability metrics** GoodCov/BadCov/BadEnt (Eq. 9–10): Fig. 3 (MH vs PH, pixel) Can 0.397/0.347 GoodCov, 0.516/0.503 BadEnt; Square 0.303/0.316, 0.658/0.572; Transport 0.248/0.217, 0.279/0.244. Fig. 4 Tool Hang epochs 800→3200: GoodCov 0.145, 0.185, 0.176, 0.157, 0.125; BadEnt 0.879, 0.747, 0.707, 0.695, 0.648; best finetuning at intermediate checkpoints.

## 5. Training recipe (paper Table 2 columns Transport / Can / Square / Tool Hang; code values from `cfg/robomimic/finetune/*/ft_distill_residual_flow_{mlp,unet_img}.yaml`)

- **Offline mixing (RLPD):** linear decay `r_offline` 0.9→0.1 / 0.7→0.1 / 0.5→0.1 / 0.5→0.1 over `T_ratio` 320k / 160k / 320k / 640k env steps. Code: `adaptive_expert_ratio_start/end/steps` with `steps: 10000` training iterations in all configs (`agent/.../train_distill_residual_flow_agent.py:200-222`). Ablation Fig. 23: removing offline data has "only a minor effect" except Tool Hang, which "degrades the final performance more noticeably" (Appendix B).
- **BC-loss filter (Eq. 6):** relax penalty iff `Q(s,a_cur) ≥ Q(s,a_pre)` and `Q(s,a_cur) − Ĝ(s) ≤ ε`; ε = −0.5 / −0.5 / −0.25 / −0.75 (Table 2). Fig. 14: largest gains on Transport and Tool Hang. **Code discrepancy:** all eight released configs set `use_soft_q_filtering: false`, which makes `bc_filter = 1` everywhere (`model/rl/distill_residual_rl.py:747-761`), i.e. the filter is off; thresholds present but unused (state −0.5/−0.5/−0.6/−0.55; img −0.5/−0.25/−0.55/−0.85). `Ĝ` in code is the replay MC return and the test is on the replayed action (`agent/...flow_agent.py:308-313`; `agent/dataset/sequence.py:265-282`).
- **BC retention:** β = 100 / 50 / 50 / 100 (Table 2; Fig. 22 β∈{50,100,150}). Code: state configs all 100; img 100/50/50/100. Code additionally masks the actor Q-term on expert samples (`disable_q_loss_for_expert_data: true`, `model/rl/distill_residual_rl.py:683-689`) and keeps BC on expert data (`always_retain_bc_loss_for_expert_data: true` except Can state) — **not described in the paper**.
- **n-step:** 3 / 3 / 3 / 5 (Table 2; configs match). Text says "n=1 works best for ... Can" (Appendix B) while Table 2 lists 3 — internal inconsistency. Critic target = mean over K next-latent candidates (Eq. 4); code uses `num_next_noise_samples: 4` for the target but `num_multi_z_for_actor_loss: 16` for the actor; paper Table 2 lists a single K = 16.
- **Warm-up:** paper has none beyond `T_ratio`. Code: `replay_flow_warmup_steps` 3000–4000 iterations of single-sample exploration before best-of-N (`model/rl/distill_residual_rl.py:539-544`), and `q_filtering_warmup_steps` 2000–5000 iterations without filtering (`:673-676`). **Not reported.**
- **Exploration/eval action selection:** best-of-N, `k* = argmax_k Q(s, π_pre(s,z_k)+s_θ(s,z_k))`, K = 16 (Algorithm 1, Table 2). Code: `online_explore_strategy` and `evaluate_strategy` both `max_q_min` with `num_exploration_samples: 16` (min over the 10 critics, then argmax, `:568-575`); `script/eval_rl_checkpoint.py:302` hard-codes `max_q_min`. Fig. 16: without best-of-N DICE-RL "still attains strong performance on all four tasks".
- **Other:** 10 critics, min; MLP [1024]×3, GELU, Adam 1e-4, τ = 0.01; gradient steps/update 10/10/20/20; parallel envs 4/4/8/8 (Table 2); batch 256 (configs); UTD ≈ 1 (README). Demos: Can 20, others 50 (§5.1). BC checkpoint chosen by early stopping (§1); configs use `state_3000/3000/4000/8000` (state) and `state_2400/2000/2400/1400` (img).
- **Checkpoint selection:** not reported. Code saves every `save_model_freq: 100` iterations and evaluates every `eval_freq: 1000` on 300 fixed-seed episodes (`num_eval_episodes × eval_n_envs` = 10×30 state, 30×10 img); curves are "averaged over 5 random seeds" (Fig. 2 caption; Appendix B).
- **Robomimic low-dim observation:** paper says only "concatenated proprioceptive state" (Appendix C). Code: `robot0_eef_pos, robot0_eef_quat, robot0_gripper_qpos` (+ `robot1_*` for Transport) **plus `object`** when no cameras (`script/dataset/process_robomimic_dataset.py:206-218`); `obs_dim` 23 Can/Square, 59 Transport, 53 Tool Hang. Object poses are included in state runs; pixel runs use proprio only plus image features.

## 6. Stated limitations

- "We do not claim that RL finetuning improves out-of-distribution generalization beyond the pretrained BC policy" (§5.3).
- Impact statement: policies "may inherit dataset biases or exhibit unsafe behavior under distribution shift".
- The design intentionally keeps exploration "largely within the prior's support" (§1); regularizer "encourages exploration within the pretrained policy's support" (§4). Residual and critic are trained only on demo and online states from the same reset distribution; no claim or test of transfer beyond it.
- Future work: VLA/multi-task finetuning "preserving behavioral diversity while achieving high-precision control"; theory for stability/sample efficiency (§6).
