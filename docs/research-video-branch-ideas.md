# Getting more out of the video (plan) branch than best-of-4 with a distilled ranker

Research memo, 2026-10-15. Inputs: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-09-plan-selection-design.md` §1–7, `docs/heldout-results.md` (last three sections), the DSRL checkout, and the papers cited by arXiv id. "Chunk cost" below means our M = 4 chunk: 4 plans × 6 video passes at batch 8 ≈ 6.7 s, ≈ 1.1 s per plan; action decode and pooling are cheap by comparison.

Facts the memo leans on: the plan is where the policy's diversity lives (across-plan action spread 0.05–0.06 vs 0.013–0.019 within a plan); V_plan ranking took task 9 from 60 to 79/100 while capturing 73% of the plan value available from 4 samples and agreeing with Q's argmax plan 47–51% of the time (chance 25%); the within-state Q gap between plans is 0.16 and the per-head regression RMSE 0.085; the sampler is a deterministic Euler flow (5 passes, CFG 5), so the plan is a deterministic function of (history, noise); the video noise is 48 × 4 × h × w per plan, about 49k dims if the 272 tokens per row are 4 × 64 patches + 16 action tokens; the post-trained model saw history noised to σ ≤ 0.5, so partially re-noised chunks are in distribution.

## 1. Noise-space RL for the video branch (DSRL-style)

Mechanism. Keep the 5B video model frozen and treat it as part of the environment: a small actor π_W(s) outputs the initial video noise w in a box [−b, b]^d, the frozen sampler maps w to a plan, and the actor is trained against a noise critic Q^W(s, w) that is regressed onto the action-space critic evaluated on the decoded result (DSRL-NA, 2506.15799 §4). For us the regression target already exists: V_plan(s, pool(plan(w))), itself distilled from Q, so the pipeline is noise → plan → pool → V_plan with no new TD learning.

What DSRL actually showed. Noise bounds b ∈ [1, 3] online; UTD 20; 2,048-wide MLPs; π0 on LIBERO about 20% → about 100% in about 10k online samples; real π0-DROID 5/20 → 18/20 in 80 episodes. On noise dimension it is explicit: π0's chunk noise is 50 × 32 = 1,600-d and they did not learn a 1,600-d policy; they trained a 32-d actor and critic and repeated the noise across the chunk (appendix). Ablations: denoising steps (training and inference) had little effect; wider base policies steered faster; DSRL-SAC needed about 2× the samples of DSRL-NA. Limitations stated by the authors and by OGPO (2605.03065): steering is bounded by base-policy coverage, fails on precision tasks where later denoising steps would need to change, crashes on some tasks despite tuning, and many noises alias to the same output. Nobody has run it above about 1.6k noise dims; 49k is 30× beyond the evidence.

Related noise-prior results. GoldenStart (ICLR 2026, GSFlow) learns a state-conditioned CVAE over the noises whose actions argmax Q among N candidates, then distils; this is amortised best-of-N and is the closest published analogue of "learn where the winning plans' noises live". Noise PPO (2506.12036) trained a full-dimensional diagonal-Gaussian noise policy (187M params, frozen SD1.5/LCM) with one-step PPO and gained only 0.2–0.3 points; gains shrank with more denoising steps. Golden Ticket (2603.15757): one constant noise vector chosen by rollouts beat Gaussian sampling in 38/43 tasks (robomimic can 43 → 81%) but no single ticket beat the base policy over a whole LIBERO suite; gains larger at 2 DDIM steps than 8. Noise hypernetworks (2508.09968): LoRA on the generator's own architecture outputs a noise perturbation with an L2 penalty standing in for the noise-space KL; recovers about half of ReNO's test-time gain at one extra forward pass.

Mapping to us. Store the M video seeds per chunk (regenerate the noise, do not store 49k floats × 4 × 20k rows). Parametrise w structurally, in increasing capacity: (i) a per-channel-per-frame mean shift (48 × 4 = 192-d) broadcast spatially, DSRL's π0 trick; (ii) that plus a low-rank spatial basis (192 × r); (iii) a bank of K learned noise tickets with a state-conditioned chooser. The critic is Q^W(s, w_lowdim) regressed to V_plan; the actor is TD3-style on Q^W with the box bound; at inference M = 1 plan from the actor, or M = 4 from the actor's Gaussian for extra safety.

Compute. Inference 1.1 s per chunk if M = 1 (6× cheaper than today); training needs plans sampled from the actor's noises (1.1 s each), which collection provides for free. Memory and code: one more small MLP pair; no gradients through the 5B model.

Risk. The 0.16 Q gap between plans may be carried by spatially local, high-frequency noise structure (which object, which grasp) that a 192-d or rank-r parametrisation cannot address; then the actor reaches nothing the prior does not already give and only loses the best-of-4 safety net. Noise aliasing makes Q^W flat over large regions. No evidence beyond 1.6k dims.

Verdict. Worth one offline test before any online run: fit Q^W on stored (s, seed) → V_plan with each parametrisation and check held-out R²; if (i) or (ii) explains a usable fraction of the within-state plan-value variance, the online actor is a small addition. The FASTER-style use of the same Q^W as a pre-screen (§2) is the lower-risk way to spend it.

## 2. Inference-time search over noise with a verifier

What the literature shows. Ma et al. (2501.09732): at about 2,880 search NFEs on FLUX/DrawBench, random best-of-N scored higher than zero-order search (ZO-2) and search-over-paths (Paths-2) under both the verifier ensemble (88.2 vs 87.3 vs 86.8) and ImageReward; first-order (gradient) search converged faster but gave "no significant margin" over zero-order at a backward cost of about 3 forwards; per-iteration returns diminish above about 50 NFEs; point-wise verifiers reduce diversity and over-search on one verifier degrades the others. The honest reading for a budget of 4: structured search does not beat random best-of-N at equal plan count; what can beat it is a cheaper verifier applied earlier. ReNO (2406.04312) does 50 gradient steps on the noise through a one-step generator (20–50 s per image) with a norm-likelihood regulariser; multi-step backprop is unstable and 100× slower. Diffusion-ES (2402.06559): gradient-free evolutionary search whose mutation is a truncated re-noise and re-denoise of elites, beating reward-gradient guidance on nuPlan.

The result that matters for us is FASTER (2604.19730): a noise-level critic Q^dn(s, ε), trained by one-step regression onto the action critic of the action that ε produces, scores N = 8 initial noises before any denoising, and only the top one is denoised. It reached parity with full best-of-N (EXPO, IDQL) on robomimic and on π0.5 (3.3B) over 5 held-out LIBERO-90 tasks at about T/2 of the compute (inference 566 → 335 ms, 8× fewer FLOPs per step); filtering at pure noise worked as well as filtering later, and a 300k-parameter critic did as well as 1.4M. This is DSRL-NA's noise critic used for selection instead of for an actor.

Mapping to us. V_noise(s, w) trained by regression onto V_plan(s, pool(plan(w))) from the stored seeds (4 per state today, 40k pairs); at inference draw 16–32 noises (free), score them with V_noise (free), denoise the top 2–4, rank those by V_plan as now. Variants: (a) M = 2 pre-screened plans at half the chunk cost; (b) M = 4 pre-screened at today's cost, which is best-of-32 under V_noise then best-of-4 under V_plan; (c) Diffusion-ES-style mutation: re-noise the V_plan winner to σ = 0.5 (in distribution for the cache), re-denoise with 2 passes, re-pool and re-rank, about one third of a plan's cost per mutant.

Compute. (a) halves the chunk; (b) unchanged; (c) +0.4 s per mutant.

Risk. Whether a 49k-d pure-noise → value map has learnable signal from 10k states is unknown; FASTER's noises are 1–2k-d chunks on small MLP policies. The structured inputs of §1 (per-channel-per-frame statistics of w, or the same low-rank projection) make this the same question as §1 and the same offline test answers both. Verifier hacking is bounded because the final choice stays with V_plan over fully denoised plans.

Verdict. Cheapest experiment in this memo and compatible with everything else.

## 3. Reward fine-tuning of the video branch with Q or V_plan as the reward

Evidence on scale and fragility. DDPO (2305.13301): full UNet fine-tune of SD1.4, 256 samples per iteration, 50k samples in 4 h on a v4-64 TPU pod; over-optimisation within 25k reward queries, checkpoints chosen by hand. DRaFT (2309.17400): reward gradient through the last sampling step with LoRA rank 8, more than 200× fewer queries than DDPO, but "eventually collapsed to producing very similar high-reward images". Flow-GRPO (2505.05470): SD3.5-M, LoRA r = 32, group G = 24 (G = 6–12 collapsed), SDE sampler with noise a = 0.7, training on 10 denoising steps vs 40 at inference, KL β = 0.01–0.04 required: without it quality fell (ImageReward 0.95 → 0.58 in the OCR run) or diversity collapsed to near-identical seeds; 24 A800s. DanceGRPO (2505.07818) on HunyuanVideo: 64 H800s, more than 10k prompts, groups of 12, and reward hacking unless all samples in a group share the same initial noise. πRL (2510.25889): π0/π0.5 with only the 300M action expert trained, Flow-SDE or learned Flow-Noise, LIBERO few-shot 57.6 → 96.1 and 77.1 → 98.3 on 8 H100s with hundreds of parallel environments; K = 1 denoising step broke training; larger chunks reduced gains. A SmolVLA PPO study (2609.28838) saw per-task collapse in 2–3 of 3 seeds under fixed or learned exploration noise. Diffusion-DPO (2311.12908): 851k human pairs on SDXL; works with AI-labelled pairs. Flow-DPO beat Flow-RWR and SFT for video (2501.13918).

Mapping to us. The reward is V_plan(s, p) or max_k Q(s, p, a_k); both are 10k-state critics, so this is reward-model over-optimisation with a weak reward model, the regime Gao et al. (2210.10760) and DRaFT describe. Our collection already produces GRPO groups: 4 plans per state with Q scores. Two routes: (i) Flow-DPO/Diffusion-DPO on within-state pairs (best vs worst plan by Q), LoRA on the video DiT, the frozen base as reference, offline on the replay; (ii) Flow-GRPO on the 4-plan groups with a KL term to the frozen base and training on 1–2 of the 5 passes. Both need the SDE or likelihood machinery on our Euler sampler (Flow-GRPO's ODE-to-SDE, or ReinFlow's learnable per-step noise, 2505.22094).

Compute. One LoRA forward+backward of the 5B model at batch 8 over 272 tokens ≈ 3 × 0.6 s ≈ 2 s per update on a GB10; memory is fine in 128 GB with activations for 1–2 passes. 10k states × 1 pair is at the floor of what worked anywhere (Gao: under about 2k comparisons an RM is at chance; Diffusion-DPO used 851k; Flow-GRPO thousands of prompts with many epochs), though our pairs are on-policy and cheap to refresh.

Risk. Moving the plan distribution moves p off Q's training support, so Q(s, p, a) must be refreshed online (we do), and the two critics chase each other; KL to the base is the only brake and it slows learning (Flow-GRPO). Loss of plan diversity removes the best-of-4 gain that currently produces most of the improvement. The 5B prior is the asset; LoRA + KL + reference anchoring makes damage reversible but not detectable without held-out evaluation at 6 h per 100 episodes.

Verdict. Mechanically feasible at LoRA scale, evidence for our data scale thin, risk high. Only after §6 raises ranker agreement, and then DPO on Q-ranked pairs before GRPO.

## 4. Value-guided sampling of the plan

Mechanism. Add ∇_z V_plan(s, pool(x̂0(z))) to the velocity at each pass, where x̂0 is the one-step clean estimate from the velocity (CGPO 2605.30056, Guided Action Flow 2607.02092), or train a time-conditioned V_plan on noised plans (Flow-NRG, 2501.13918: reward model on noisy latents with shared noise across the pair, BT loss, guidance w = 100 at every step).

Evidence. GAF: 0.45B VLA with a 2.7M critic, 60.0 → 82.5% over six real tasks, no best-of-N comparison. CGPO: guidance only in the last 10 steps, spherical-Gaussian constraint and truncated-quantile critic to stop off-manifold drift, and it never deploys guidance at inference; the guided samples are training targets. Flow-NRG: trades text alignment for visual quality as the weights shift (TA win rate 26.7% at 0.5:0.5:0), i.e. hacking shows up at inference as well. Ma et al.: first-order search no better than zero-order; Diffusion-ES beat gradient guidance with gradient-free mutation.

Mapping and cost. The gradient needs a backward pass through the pooling transformer pass (batch M, empty cache), about 3× a forward, per guided pass; guiding all 5 passes roughly doubles the chunk cost at M = 4. Guiding only the last pass (σ 0.56 → 0) with the x̂0 pool is about +15%.

Risk. V_plan's gradient in 3,072-d pool space is its least trustworthy output: an overfit regressor's gradient is an adversarial direction, and every offline refit overfit. The within-plan facts say a 4-sample random search already captures most of what a local step would.

Verdict. Low priority. If anything, the gradient-free mutation of §2(c) is the in-distribution version of this idea.

## 5. Video-world-model planning with a value function

UniPi (2302.00111): text-to-video plans plus inverse dynamics, no value, no selection. VLP (2310.10625): beam search over video samples scored by a 12B PaLM-E "steps to go" value; plan quality 4% → 56% as beams/branching grow, execution 0 → 16% on make-line, about 30 min per plan on a 4-TPU pod; they clip the heuristic to stop the search exploiting the value model. VPP (2412.14803), GR-2 (2410.06158), GR-MG (2408.14368), WorldVLA (2506.21539): the predicted future is a representation or co-training signal; no value function, no selection. DINO-WM (2411.04983) and V-JEPA 2 plan by CEM over action sequences scored by goal-feature distance, which needs an action-conditioned predictor; our video branch predicts before the actions, so this does not transfer directly. Dreamer-family: critic learned in imagination, actor-critic, not selection. VideoAgent (2410.10076): refines plans with VLM feedback and self-conditioning consistency. ViVa (2604.08168), World Value Model (2606.24742), World-Value-Action (2604.14732): the value head is put on the video model's own features or the generator is retrained to emit values; they report that this beats external value models on temporal/physical judgement.

Takeaways. Only VLP quantifies selection vs budget, and its lesson is that value-guided search over video samples works but the value model gets exploited (hence the clip), which is the §6 problem again. The ViVa/WVM line is the strongest evidence that plan-value features should come from inside the video model rather than from a mean pool fed to an MLP.

## 6. Making the distilled plan ranker generalise from ~10k states

Where we are. Agreement 47–51% and 73% value captured; targets are Q with within-state max−min 0.16; heads have RMSE 0.085; inputs are 3,072-d mean pools; 10,278 states × 4 plans; every offline refit (MSE, Polyak, state-centred, pairwise, pool − state) overfits and underperforms the online ranker.

(e) first: is 73% near the ceiling? Simulation with 4 candidates whose true values are Gaussian with std σ_v and ranker errors of std σ_e (independent):

| σ_e / σ_v | argmax agreement | value captured |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.74 | 0.89 |
| 0.75 | 0.65 | 0.80 |
| 1.0 | 0.58 | 0.71 |
| 1.25 | 0.53 | 0.63 |
| 1.5 | 0.49 | 0.56 |

Max − min of 4 Gaussians is 2.06 σ_v, so σ_v ≈ 0.16 / 2.06 ≈ 0.078. The observed 73% captured sits at σ_e / σ_v ≈ 1.0, i.e. an effective ranking error of about 0.08, the single-head RMSE: the 10-head min-ensemble is not averaging errors down, as expected when heads share data and inputs. Agreement of 0.5 sits at ratio 1.4, so the ranker gets the large gaps right and loses the near-ties; both numbers are far from any ceiling against Q. The ceiling that matters is against the true value, and Q's own error sets it. Two measurements give it without new data: (1) split-half reliability: rank the stored plans with heads 1–5 and with heads 6–10 and measure their argmax agreement and mutual value captured; a ranker regressed on one half cannot beat that against the other, and Spearman–Brown converts the half-ensemble figure to the full one; (2) on the 10% random-plan chunks, Q's argmax plan vs the realised Monte-Carlo return (the regret actually paid). If split-half agreement is under about 0.6, V_plan is within noise of the ceiling and the fix is Q, not V_plan.

(a) Representation. The ranker study is the picture of a 3,072-d input with 10k examples: train fit 0.94–1.00, test 0.41–0.62. Options with evidence: per-frame pools (4 × 3,072) through a shared learned projection to 128–256 dims with LayerNorm, weight decay and dropout (BRO, 2405.16158: LayerNorm after every dense layer is "essential", 5M-parameter critics, AdamW); frozen features from inside the model: Farebrother et al. (2403.03950) show cross-entropy-trained value nets give better linear-probe features, and ViVa/WVA/VPP put the value or policy on the video model's internal activations rather than an output pool. Our cheapest in-model feature is the action branch's cross-attention readout of the plan (what the decoder actually consumes), available during the decode we already run. Contrastive or predictive pretraining of plan features from the replay (plan pool → next-state pool or → return) is the SPR/DR3-style route; DR3 (2112.04716) warns that bootstrapped value features degenerate, but ours are regressed, so a fixed predictive projection trained once and frozen is the safe version.

(b) More supervision per state. All M × K = 16 Q values per state are already stored; regress V_plan on a per-candidate head or on (mean_k, max_k) rather than the single max, so each state gives 16 targets and the max is read off the output. More plans per state at collection (M = 8 costs 2× video time) adds correlated targets; cheaper augmentation is the §2(c) mutation: re-noise a stored plan to σ ≤ 0.5, re-denoise with 2 passes, decode and pool (about 0.4 s per extra plan against 1.1 s), giving in-distribution neighbours with Q targets, the generative-augmentation idea of GTA (2405.16907) with the frozen model as the generator. GoldenStart uses N_cand candidates per state for exactly this, and Cobbe et al. (2110.14168) trained their verifier on 100 samples per problem and found more unique samples, not more epochs, is what helps (Gao et al.: under about 2k comparisons an RM stays at chance; more epochs on small data do not help).

(c) Regime. Cobbe: solution-level verifiers overfit within an epoch, dropout "significantly mitigates" it, token-level (dense) supervision overfits less and keeps improving; Gao: unique data beats epochs. BRO: LayerNorm + weight decay + periodic full resets. Farebrother: HL-Gauss (histogram cross-entropy on a Gaussian-smoothed target, σ/bin ≈ 0.75, 51–101 bins) over MSE gave 67% higher peak on Q-Transformer manipulation, 1.8–2.1× on multi-task Atari, with the advantage largest under noisy targets and gone when targets are deterministic; our targets are noisy critic outputs, which is their favourable case, though their data scales are far larger. Bagged heads on disjoint state subsets with a conservative combination (Coste et al. 2310.02743: conservative ensembles practically eliminate over-optimisation in best-of-N, up to +70%) replace the current ten heads on the same data. Mixup for regression (C-Mixup, 2210.05775) and input-noise injection on the pool are standard for 10k-example regression. Early stopping on the online stream is implicit today and is the reason the live ranker beats every refit; a held-out state split with early stopping should be built into the online update.

(d) Comparator rather than value. Evidence that pairwise beats pointwise at this scale is weak: RoboMonkey (2506.17811) found Bradley-Terry with a margin term beat RMSE regression by 6% action error out of distribution, but on 10^4–2×10^7 synthetic pairs; HelpSteer2-Preference (2410.01257) matched BT and regression on about 10k pairs and found them close, with the combination best; our own pairwise refit overfit worse than MSE. A siamese comparator on (s, p_i, p_j) adds a BT head beside the regression head (the HelpSteer2-Preference combination) rather than replacing it; listwise over the 4 plans is the same information. Do not expect the loss to fix generalisation; the study already showed it does not.

(f) From RM/verifier practice. Verifier gains scale with unique samples and appear only above a data threshold (Cobbe, Gao); best-of-N is more KL-efficient than RL and over-optimises later (Gao), which supports keeping selection rather than fine-tuning; ensembles plus conservative combination control exploitation (Coste); keep the candidate count moderate because V-GPS (2410.13816) saw success rise to K = 50 and fall at K = 100 as the value got exploited, and VLP clipped its heuristic for the same reason; Q-ranking over 16 candidates doing worse than V_plan over 4 (74 vs 79) is our own instance.

Ranked changes most likely to raise held-out agreement:

1. Lower-dimensional in-model features with regularisation: per-frame pools or the action branch's plan readout → learned 128–256-d projection, LayerNorm, weight decay, dropout, input noise; bagged heads on disjoint states with early stopping on a held-out split. The study shows the failure is input dimension vs examples, and this attacks it directly. No literature number at our scale; BRO and Cobbe give the direction.
2. Classification targets on all 16 candidates per state: HL-Gauss over the Q range, 16 targets per state. Documented effect: +67% peak on Q-Transformer, 1.8–2.1× multi-task Atari, largest under noisy targets; expect a fraction of that here.
3. Cheap plan augmentation: 2–4 re-noised neighbours per stored plan (0.4 s each, offline on the second GB10) with Q targets, doubling or tripling unique supervision. Cobbe/Gao: unique samples are the lever; GoldenStart and GTA show the mechanism.

Measure the ceiling (e) before any of these; if split-half Q agreement is near 0.5, spend the effort on Q (more heads on disjoint data, HL-Gauss on Q too).

## Ranked top three for us

1. Noise-level pre-screen (§2, FASTER/DSRL-NA): V_noise(s, w) on structured noise statistics regressed onto V_plan from stored seeds; halve the chunk or screen 32 noises for the same cost. Testable offline on the current buffer, no gradients through the 5B model, and it is the first step of a DSRL-style noise actor if the regression has signal.
2. Ranker (§6): in-model low-dim features, HL-Gauss on 16 targets per state, bagged early-stopped heads, after measuring the Q ceiling. Every other method in this memo optimises against V_plan or Q, so their value is bounded by this.
3. Flow-DPO with LoRA on the video DiT using within-state plan pairs ranked by Q, KL/reference-anchored, best-of-4 kept (§3). The only method here that contracts the sampler itself; gated behind 1 and 2 because its reward is the ranker.

Not recommended now: gradient guidance of the plan (§4), full-parameter or GRPO fine-tuning without a trusted reward, and a full-dimensional noise actor (§1 without structure).

## Three papers to read in full

- FASTER, 2604.19730: noise-level critic that matches best-of-N at a fraction of the compute, including on a 3.3B VLA.
- Farebrother et al., "Stop Regressing", 2403.03950: classification losses for value functions and why they help under noisy targets.
- Flow-GRPO, 2505.05470: the practical recipe (SDE conversion, few training steps, KL) if the video branch is ever fine-tuned; read with DSRL 2506.15799's appendix (already local) for the structured-noise trick.
